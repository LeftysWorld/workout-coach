## Body weight and measurements

Measurements say whether the weight lost is fat or muscle. Read them together with strength:

| Waist | Arms, chest, shoulders | Strength | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| down | holding or up | holding or up | Losing fat, keeping muscle. The goal while cutting. |
| down | down a little | holding | Normal while cutting. Watch it. |
| down | down | falling | Possibly losing muscle. The cut may be too fast: say so; don't add volume. |
| flat | up | up | Building muscle at a steady weight. |
| up | up | up | Gaining, mostly muscle if strength climbs too. |

**Pace of a cut:** about 0.5–1% of body weight a week keeps strength. Faster (`bodyweight_loss_pct_per_week`
over 1) makes stalls and fatigue more likely: expect lighter weeks sooner and don't read a stall as
a weak lift.

**Noise:** day-to-day weight swings by a few pounds; use the weekly averages and the 4-week trend.
Tape measurements move slowly: a change under about a quarter inch in 4 weeks is within measuring
error. Never read a single reading.

**No diagnosis, no diet plan.** Say what the numbers show and what it means for training ("arms
shrinking while lifts fall: the cut may be too fast"). Food is outside this coach.
