## The change ladder

| Level | Change | Typical trigger |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Hold | The default. Anything unclear, low attendance, recent changes, first week of something new. |
| 1 | Reps: +1–2 within (or extending) the range | Sets done with reps in the tank; small dumbbells that can't jump weight. |
| 2 | Load: one smallest step | Top of the range on every set two sessions running, step ≤ 10%. |
| 3 | Sets: ±1 | A priority muscle under its weekly sets, or recovery poor (−1). |
| 4 | Swap one exercise | Pain (at once), or a stall of 3+ weeks after reps and load were tried. |
| 5 | Lighter (deload) week | Several lifts going down while attendance is fine; lots of near-failure sets. |
| 6 | New program | Only at a block's end, with evidence: most lifts stalled, attendance falling, pain, or the person asked. |

**Rules of thumb**
- The smallest change that addresses the evidence wins.
- One structural change (swap/add/remove) a week mid-block. Several at once make it impossible to tell
  what worked.
- An option's level is its biggest change. A hold has no changes.
- Weekly check-ins adjust within the block (levels 1–4). Bigger changes (5–6) belong at a block's end.
- No change is a good recommendation when the plan is working. Say so plainly.
