## Program blocks

- **A month at a time.** The program runs in 4-week blocks counted from its last structural change
  (`block.week_in_block`). Weekly check-ins make small adjustments; the last week of a block is when
  bigger changes are considered.
- **"The body gets used to it" is not a reason.** For a beginner, adding reps and weight to the same
  lifts is what drives progress. Changing exercises resets that progress and the learning curve.
- **Good reasons for a new program** (level 6), at a block's end:
  - most lifts stalled (3+ weeks) despite steady training and reps/load/sets already tried
  - training dropping off over several weeks and the person's notes point at the plan (boredom, too long)
  - pain that the current exercises keep causing
  - the person asked (`requested_program_change: true`) or a planned replacement date has come
  - the goal changed
- **A calendar trigger only raises the question.** After 2–3 blocks on one program, the analyst should
  check for the reasons above. No reasons, no new program.
- **Deload (level 5)**: see the fatigue-and-deload skill. Evidence, not the calendar.
- **Rotating one exercise** (before it goes stale, not on a timer): after about 6–12 weeks on it
  (`weeks_on_exercise`), if it has stalled after reps and load were tried, or the notes say it's
  boring or uncomfortable, swap it for a variation in the same pattern. Keep the main lifts
  (presses, rows, pulldowns) longest: their progress is the clearest record of getting stronger.
- **History matters.** If the program changed in the last 4 weeks, another big change needs pain or
  the person's request.
